Quality, Hygiene & Compliance

Food Safety Guidelines & Checklist

Protecting the health and dignity of recipients is paramount. All donors and recipient organizations must strictly follow these food safety rules.

Platform Safety & Liability Notice

FoodBridge provides digital coordination between food businesses and registered charities. The platform does not certify food safety on-site. Donors and recipient organizations must maintain their own standard operating procedures, comply with applicable public health regulations (such as the Bill Emerson Good Samaritan Food Donation Act / local food safety authorities), and inspect all food prior to collection and consumption.

Temperature Holding Standards

Pathogenic bacteria multiply rapidly between 4°C and 60°C (40°F – 140°F). Keep food strictly out of the danger zone.

Cold Holding: Below 4°C (40°F)

Refrigerate dairy, raw produce, cut fruits, and chilled prepared dishes immediately. Keep cold during transport using ice packs or refrigerated vans.

Danger Zone: 4°C – 60°C

Never leave cooked perishable food at ambient room temperature for more than 2 hours. If left in ambient temperatures beyond safe limits, dispose responsibly.

Hot Holding: Above 60°C (140°F)

Maintain hot catered foods in steam tables or insulated thermal carriers until handoff. Ensure core food temperature does not drop below 60°C.

Donor Verification Checklist (Before Publishing)

  • 1. Accurately Record Preparation Date & Time

    Specify exactly when the batch was finished. Do not estimate or list items prepared more than 4 hours prior without rapid chilling.

  • 2. Food-Grade Packaging & Clean Covers

    Pack food in sterile aluminium trays, clean food-grade buckets, or sealed boxes. Never use trash bags, contaminated buckets, or unsealed containers.

  • 3. Explicit Allergen Disclosure

    Clearly declare if the donation contains any of the top allergens: peanuts, tree nuts, milk/dairy, eggs, wheat/gluten, soy, fish, or shellfish.

  • 4. Realistic Pickup Deadline

    Establish a safe pickup window that gives recipient charities sufficient time to transport and serve the food before microbial degradation.

  • 5. Prohibited Items: What NEVER to Donate

    Never donate food that was served on a customer's individual plate (plate scrape), items with off-odors or signs of spoilage, unpasteurized milk, swollen/rusted cans, or items left uncovered in public buffet stations.

Recipient NGO Transport & Serving Checklist

  • 1. Insulated Transport Containers

    Arrive at the donor pickup site with clean, sanitized insulated bags, coolers with ice packs, or heated Cambro carriers to prevent temperature abuse.

  • 2. Visual & Sensory Inspection Upon Collection

    Examine containers for intact seals, proper temperatures, and hygiene before accepting handoff.

  • 3. Prompt Distribution & Reheating

    Distribute to beneficiaries immediately upon arrival. If reheating cooked foods, rapidly heat to an internal temperature of at least 74°C (165°F) for 15 seconds.
